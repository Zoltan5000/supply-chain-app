    How confidence works

    • Official government lists count most. A match on the UFLPA Entity List, a CBP order, or North Korean labor enforcement sets the result to red.
    • Verified beats estimated. Records traced in shipment data and checked by a person count most.
    • Two sources agreeing raises confidence. When Sayari and Tradeverifyd both show the same supplier or flag, the result is stronger.
    • Disagreement goes to a person. If the two sources conflict, the result is flagged for human review.
    • Not checked isn't clean. A company that hasn't been screened shows as unknown, never as "no problems found."

        The UFLPA Entity List

        The single most important list for cotton apparel. A match here turns a result red automatically.

        187companies listed since Aug 3, 2026
        43added July 31, 2026, the largest expansion yet
        24,300+shipments denied entry, worth nearly $1 billion

        What the law does

        The Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act was signed December 23, 2021. Since June 21, 2022, US customs presumes that goods made wholly or in part in China's Xinjiang region, or by any company on the Entity List, were made with forced labor and bars them from entering the US.

        Importers can only overcome this with "clear and convincing evidence." There's no minimum amount: any traceable input from a listed company can get a whole shipment detained.

        For apparel this matters because Xinjiang grows most of China's cotton, so cotton can pass through several countries and still trace back to the region.

        Who ends up on the list

        • Makers in Xinjiang that mine, produce or manufacture goods with forced labor.
        • Companies that recruit or move workers out of Xinjiang with the regional government, including Uyghurs, Kazakhs and Kyrgyz people.
        • Exporters of those goods into the US.
        • Companies sourcing from Xinjiang through government "poverty alleviation" or "pairing assistance" labor programs.

        The July 2026 additions focused on aluminum, apparel, copper, cotton, and tomatoes.

        How the app will use it

        • Check every tier, not just the factory. A listed spinner or cotton trader three tiers up still counts.
        • Match aliases and former names. Many entries list several names, and some cover all "subsidiaries" or "affiliated entities."
        • Flag Xinjiang origin even without a listed company, since the presumption covers the whole region.
        • Refresh automatically whenever the list changes, and show the date it was last checked.
        • A confirmed match sets the result to red. A likely-but-unconfirmed match goes to human review first.

        Submit a source

        Anyone can add evidence: a report, an article, a court record, a worker's account. Submissions start with low confidence and only count toward a score once they're checked.

        Why submissions count for less at first

        Anyone can submit anything, including companies trying to boost their own score or competitors trying to hurt a rival. So every submission moves up a confidence ladder as it's checked:

        0.3Submitted, not checked. Visible to our team only. Doesn't change public scores.
        0.5–0.7Checked by the AI agent and backed by other data. Can count as industry or country context.
        0.85–1.0Verified by a person. Only now can a claim about a specific supplier affect a score.

          Rules that keep this fair

          • No submission can turn a result red on its own. Only verified evidence can.
          • Good news needs proof too. Claims that improve a score are checked just as strictly.
          • Submitters are protected. Identities stay private, and anonymous submissions are allowed.
          • Submissions are data, never instructions. The agent only extracts claims and ignores anything in a document that tries to tell it what to do.
